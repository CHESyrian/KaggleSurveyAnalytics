# India Deep-Dive: Kaggle Survey 2020 → 2022

**Why this report:** India rose from **29% → 37%** of cleaned responses (+7.4 percentage points) and is the main driver of sample composition change. Global averages are increasingly India-weighted.

| Year | India n | Rest of world n | India share |
|------|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| 2020 | 5,572 | 13,426 | 29.3% |
| 2022 | 8,528 | 14,707 | 36.7% |

---

## 1. Age: much younger than the rest of the world

| Group (2022) | Age 18–24 | Implication |
|--------------|-----------|-------------|
| **India** | **57%** | Student / early-career heavy |
| Rest of world | 26% | Closer to mid-career mix |

- In 2020 the gap was similar (India 61% aged 18–24 vs Rest 26%).  
- India’s growth therefore **pulls the global sample younger**, which helps explain the rise of the 18–21 band overall.

---

## 2. Education: more Bachelor’s, fewer advanced degrees

**2022**

| Degree | India | Rest of world |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| Bachelor’s | **41%** | 27% |
| Master’s | 35% | **40%** |
| Doctoral | ~7% | higher share outside India |

India’s respondents are earlier in the formal-education pipeline on average. Master’s is still large, but Bachelor’s is the modal category in India (unlike the global modal Master’s).

---

## 3. Gender

| Year | Women % India | Women % Rest |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| 2020 | 22% | 18% |
| 2022 | **25%** | 20% |

India’s sample is slightly **more gender-balanced** than the rest of the world in both years. Part of the global rise in women’s share (19% → 22%) is composition from India’s growth and India’s own improvement.

---

## 4. Experience: more early-stage ML

| Metric (approx.) | India | Rest of world |
|------------------|-------|---------------|
| Under 1 year ML (2020) | 40% | 28% |
| Under 1 year ML (2022) | 37% | 26% |

India contributes a larger share of **learners**. Product and content strategies that ignore this will mis-target the single biggest country segment.

---

## 5. Tools: Python-first, less R, similar SQL

**2022 regular-use rates**

| Tool | India | Rest of world |
|------|-------|---------------|
| Python | 78% | 78% |
| SQL | 39% | 41% |
| R | **14%** | **22%** |
| Scikit-learn | 46% | 49% |
| TensorFlow | 33% | 34% |
| PyTorch | **17%** | **24%** |

- Python is equally dominant.  
- **R is notably weaker in India.**  
- PyTorch adoption lags the rest of the world; TensorFlow is similar.  
- Classical ML (Scikit-learn) is slightly lower in India in 2022, consistent with a younger learner mix.

---

## 6. What this means for interpretation

1. **Any global KPI** (age, education, early ML, R use, PyTorch) is partly an India composition effect. Always check **India vs Rest** before declaring a worldwide trend.  
2. **Curriculum for India** can emphasize Bachelor’s-level and early-career paths, Python + SQL, and accessible classical ML, with growing but still secondary deep-learning content.  
3. **Community programs** in India have a younger, slightly more gender-balanced base than the non-India sample — design events and mentorship accordingly.  
4. **Rest-of-world trends** (e.g. PyTorch gain, older age mix) are clearer when India is held separate.

---

## 7. Caveats

- Country is self-reported; “India” is the selected choice, not verified residency.  
- Kaggle’s India user base growth may differ from growth in the broader Indian data-science workforce.  
- Role harmonization is weaker in 2022; country-level role comparisons should use caution.
