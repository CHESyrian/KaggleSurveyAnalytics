# Gender Gap Briefing: Kaggle Survey 2020 → 2022

**Purpose:** Clear, caveat-aware view of gender patterns in the Kaggle sample — overall share, age, roles, and tools.  
**Important:** These are **descriptive patterns in a self-selected sample**, not causal estimates and not representative of the global workforce.

---

## 1. Headline share

| Year | Men | Women | Prefer not to say / other |
|------|-----|-------|---------------------------|
| 2020 | 79% | **19%** | ~2% |
| 2022 | 76% | **22%** | ~2% |

- Women’s share rose about **3 percentage points**.  
- The gap remains large (~3.5:1 men to women in 2022).  
- Improvement is real but slow relative to the size of the imbalance.

---

## 2. Age: gap is smaller at entry level

**Women’s share by age (2022)**

| Age | Women % |
|-----|---------|
| 18–21 | **26%** |
| 22–24 | **25%** |
| 25–29 | 22% |
| 30–34 | 22% |
| 35–39 | 23% |
| 40–44 | 21% |

Younger cohorts are more balanced. If this reflects a pipeline change (not only survey mix), representation may improve over time as these cohorts age — but only if retention into senior roles holds.

---

## 3. Roles: representation differs sharply by title

**2020 (clearest role labels)**

| Role | Women % | n |
|------|---------|---|
| Student | **24%** | 4,912 |
| Data Analyst | **24%** | 1,397 |
| Research Scientist | 20% | 1,114 |
| Data Scientist | 16% | 2,554 |
| Software Engineer | 13% | 1,873 |
| ML Engineer | **12%** | 1,030 |

**2022 (clear titles only)**

| Role | Women % | n |
|------|---------|---|
| Academic / Teacher | **32%** | 808 |
| Research Scientist | 18% | 573 |
| Data Scientist | 16% | 1,861 |
| Software Engineer | 14% | 949 |
| Manager / Executive | 12% | 796 |
| ML Engineer | **10%** | 546 |

**Pattern:** Higher share in education, student, and analyst paths; lower in ML Engineer and (to a lesser extent) Software Engineer / Manager titles.

---

## 4. Tools: gaps in regular use (2022)

| Tool | Men | Women | Gap (pp) |
|------|-----|-------|----------|
| Python | 80% | 70% | −10 |
| Scikit-learn | 50% | 39% | −11 |
| TensorFlow | 35% | 26% | −9 |
| PyTorch | 24% | 14% | −10 |

Women in the sample report lower regular use of core languages and frameworks. This likely mixes:
- Different role mix (more analysts/students vs ML engineers)
- Experience differences  
- Self-selection into the survey  

It should **not** be read as “women use tools less by nature.” Role-adjusted analysis would be needed for stronger claims.

---

## 5. Geography note

India’s sample is slightly more gender-balanced than the rest of the world (2022: ~25% vs ~20% women). Because India grew as a share of responses, part of the global improvement in women’s share is a **composition effect**.

---

## 6. “Never coded” group

In 2022, among respondents who report never having written code (~8.5% of sample), women are **~35%** — much closer to parity than the overall survey. This group may include business/analytics entrants exploring data science without a coding background.

---

## 7. What this briefing supports

| Use case | Suggested action |
|----------|------------------|
| Community programs | Target ML Engineer / SE paths where gaps are widest; sustain student/analyst gains |
| Curriculum | Ensure early Python/SQL/sklearn on-ramps are welcoming; measure completion by gender where possible |
| Reporting | Always show gender **by role and age**, not only headline % |
| Research | Do not treat raw tool gaps as discrimination metrics without role/experience adjustment |

---

## 8. Caveats (read before sharing)

1. Self-selected Kaggle users — not a labor-force survey.  
2. Gender is self-reported with limited categories.  
3. Role definitions changed between 2020 and 2022.  
4. Tool gaps are unadjusted for role and experience.  
5. No claims about hiring bias, ability, or interest are supported by this data alone.
