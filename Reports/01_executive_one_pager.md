# Executive One-Pager: Kaggle Survey 2020 → 2022

**Audience:** Leadership, product, curriculum, and community leads  
**Data:** Cleaned Kaggle ML & DS Survey responses (~19k in 2020, ~23k in 2022)

---

## Five headline metrics

| KPI | 2020 | 2022 | Direction |
|-----|------|------|-----------|
| Python regular use | 78% | 78% | Stable — still the default language |
| Women in sample | 19% | 22% | Slight improvement; large gap remains |
| India share of responses | 29% | 37% | Sharp rise; drives sample composition |
| Under 1 year ML experience | ~44%* | ~44%* | Stable — learner-heavy community |
| PyTorch vs TensorFlow use | 21% / 35% | 22% / 33% | Mild shift toward PyTorch |

\*Including early-career ML bands; share with very limited ML tenure remains the majority of the sample.

---

## Three charts worth showing

1. **Age mix** — 18–21 becomes the largest band in 2022; the community got younger.  
2. **Language & framework bars** — Python dominant; SQL up; Scikit-learn still #1 framework; TF/Keras down, PyTorch up slightly.  
3. **Country share** — India +7 pp; top-10 concentration increased (66% → 71% of responses).

---

## Three implications

1. **Curriculum & content**  
   Keep Python + SQL at the center. Maintain Scikit-learn as the primary classical-ML path. Increase PyTorch coverage relative to TensorFlow/Keras. Design explicitly for learners (large under-1-year ML cohort).

2. **Community & geography**  
   India is now more than a third of responses and is much younger and more Bachelor’s-heavy than the rest of the world. Global “average” metrics are increasingly India-weighted — segment analyses by region before generalizing.

3. **Representation**  
   Women’s share improved modestly overall and is higher among students and analysts than among ML Engineer / Software Engineer titles. Progress is real but uneven; role-level gaps matter more than the headline percentage alone.

---

## What not to over-claim

- Sample is self-selected Kaggle users — not the global data-science workforce.  
- Compensation is too incomplete (~46–66% missing) for salary decisions.  
- Role labels changed between years; treat role YoY shifts carefully.
